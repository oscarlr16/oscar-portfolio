Real-time embedded control for a robotic espresso machine

Professional project at Meticulous Home, Inc. · C++, FreeRTOS, cascaded PID

Not just a coffee maker

At first glance, an espresso machine looks like a conventional appliance. The Meticulous Espresso is a highly constrained, multi-variable robotic system: the world's first robotic lever espresso machine. A custom embedded architecture on FreeRTOS manages up to 10 high-precision sensors in real time. Instead of a traditional pump, a high-torque motor and cascaded PID loops drive water at 9 bars while tracking fluid flow, 0.1 g weight variations and fast thermal changes within milliseconds. It is a coupled fluid-dynamics and thermal control problem solved on the fly.

I worked hands-on with embedded C++ and FreeRTOS to bring the system from early prototypes to mass production. I took part in implementing a cascaded control architecture in which flow or speed tracking algorithms generate position setpoints for an internal motor controller. I also co-developed real-time algorithms that compensate for physical non-linearities, such as motor stiction and load cell creep, keeping the extraction metrics stable.

Why is this interesting?

Managing real-time concurrency in an RTOS, filtering noisy multi-sensor data and tuning cascaded loops around unpredictable hardware are the same problems as stabilizing a drone in turbulent wind. I'm used to bridging control theory and low-level hardware constraints, and I want to bring that hands-on C/C++ rigor to STM32 flight controllers.

Nonlinear control and simulation of a 3-DOF Delta parallel manipulator

Academic project · MATLAB, Simulink, Simscape Multibody, SolidWorks

In my last semester I wanted to go beyond standard PID and LQR and tackle a nonlinear system. The parallel architecture of a Delta robot caught my attention, but when COVID-19 hit, building a physical prototype was impossible. The challenge became modeling the physical constraints accurately in software, so I built a Software-in-the-Loop setup that connects a custom SolidWorks CAD design to MATLAB's Simscape Multibody.

Out of curiosity, I built the math from scratch instead of relying on black-box tools. I derived the inverse kinematics with spatial analytical geometry to map 3D Cartesian trajectories to motor angles. Then I formulated the coupled dynamics by hand, computing kinetic and potential energy with the Euler-Lagrange formulation to understand how inertia and gravity shape the robot's motion.

Why is this interesting?

Stabilizing highly coupled dynamics and compensating for gravity analytically carries directly to flight control algorithms for UAVs. The same simulation-first approach, validating in software-in-the-loop before touching hardware, is how I would want to test flight code before it flies.
